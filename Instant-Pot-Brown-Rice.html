Why not a flat ratio: the pot loses a roughly fixed amount of water to steam while coming up to pressure and releasing. That loss is a big share of the water at 1 cup of rice and a trivial share at 5, so the ratio starts near 1:1¼ and settles toward 1:1 as the batch grows. Time at pressure never changes; only the time to reach pressure does. 6-quart pot; add a few minutes to the come-to-pressure time in an 8-quart.

    Method

    1. Rinse and drainRinse the rice in a fine-mesh strainer until the water runs mostly clear, then shake it dry for 30 seconds. The water in the table assumes drained rice; unrinsed rice gets about 2 tablespoons less water per cup.
    2. Load the potAdd the rice, 2¼ cups of water, the salt and the oil to the inner pot. Give it one stir so no grains sit above the waterline, then wipe the rim. Never fill past the halfway line; rice expands.
    3. Pressure cookLock the lid, set the valve to Sealing, and cook on High Pressure (Manual / Pressure Cook) for 20 minutes. Expect roughly 10 minutes to reach pressure before the countdown starts.
    4. Natural releaseWhen the timer ends, leave it alone for 10 minutes, then turn the valve to Venting to release the rest. Opening early leaves the centers firm.
    5. Fluff and restFluff with a fork, then leave the lid cracked for 2 minutes so the surface steam escapes and the grains stay separate. Yields about 6 cups.

    Notes & Tips

    Texture dial. 20 minutes gives fully cooked, slightly chewy grains. For softer rice use 22 minutes and add 2 tablespoons of water per cup; for firmer, 17 minutes. Don't push water past 1:1½ or it turns wet and flat-tasting.

    Short-grain and brown basmati. Same time and water. Wild rice and rice blends are different and need their own times.

    Less than 1 cup. Below 1 cup the pot can't hold its minimum liquid without drowning the rice. Cook pot-in-pot instead: rice and water in a heat-safe bowl on the trivet, 1 cup of water in the base, same time plus 5 minutes.

    Burn notice. Almost always a dirty sealing ring, a valve left on Venting, or unrinsed starchy rice stuck to the bottom. Rinse, stir once, and check the ring.

    Storage. Cooled rice keeps 4 days in the fridge or 3 months frozen in flat bags. Reheat with a splash of water.
